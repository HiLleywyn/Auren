Broadband.
Without Satellites.

A rural network built around adjustable ground relays.

The idea is simple: raise the radios where they can reach more homes, keep another route available, and bring the equipment down when it needs repair. All internet connections stay on the ground.

The Network

The Relay Design

In a rural network, towers, site work, and specialist climbing can cost more than the radios. We’re testing whether reusable structures and equipment serviced from the ground could lower the total bill.

Radio links connect homes to the relays. Fiber or other terrestrial links connect the network to the internet. Optical links may add capacity between relays. The controller model checks spare capacity before planning a height change or maintenance.

Deployable masts, mesh networks, and optical links already exist. The part we want to test is how a repairable mast and coordinated routing work together.

Build And Repair
At Ground Level

The mast would stay supported by mechanical locks while a separate carriage brings the radios down for repair. The first prototype needs to prove that arrangement is safe and useful.

See The Mast Design ↗

Ground-Level Assembly

Short mast sections would be added at the base. Alternating collars would carry the load while the next section is fitted. This needs a restrained prototype and independent engineering review.

Coverage

Where It Could Work

First Deployment

Open rural terrain, clustered homes, and two independent terrestrial internet handoffs. Start with qualified 2–5 km paths. Test the mast mechanism against a conventional installation.

What We Do Not Claim

This design does not solve dense forest, open-ocean, or aviation coverage. It has not demonstrated simultaneous 100 Mbps service to every home or 99.9% availability. Patentability has not been established.

The Longer Horizon

Stratospheric relays could extend coverage without satellites. We still need answers on year-round power, station-keeping, spectrum, capacity, and fleet cost. Global coverage remains a research goal.

Next Steps

Development Plan

  1. 30 days

    Survey The Sites

    Survey twenty homes with a rented certified mast. Get real upstream quotes. Build a low-height locking/lifting rig.

  2. 90 days

    Test The Mechanism

    Two experimental relays. Paid field work. Congestion, failures, battery endurance, and maintenance measured against a control network.

  3. One year

    Run A Community Pilot

    Structural qualification, seasonal data, a 100–240-home pilot, and independently reviewed lifecycle costs.
