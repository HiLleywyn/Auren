# Five-year economics and commercial gates

Revision 0.1; estimated US dollars, price reference checked 2026-10-14. No supplier bids, lender quote or Auren operating data have been obtained. No donated labor or subsidies assumed.

## Accounting boundary

Compare the spend to serve households using purchased Starlink service against fully allocated Auren deployment costs. Starlink internal provider cost is unpublished; no claim of a 75% lower internal provider cost is made. Keep retail customer prices separate.

Reference service: up to 100/20 Mbps household service, unlimited data, useful busy-hour performance and measured comparable availability. All equivalence remains unproven. Comparing a 100 Mbps Auren design to Starlink Max solely to obtain a favorable percentage is invalid.

## Community baseline: 240 continuously subscribed homes

| Five-year shared item | USD |
| --- | ---: |
| Infrastructure, gateways, structures, site power and installation | 30,000 |
| Permits/compliance allowance | 2,000 |
| Two upstreams total $700/month | 42,000 |
| Site payments | 3,600 |
| Shared electricity | 1,500 |
| Paid field/operations allocation | 37,500 |
| Shared maintenance/replacements | 10,000 |
| Insurance | 3,000 |
| Monitoring/admin | 500 |
| Financing allowance | 6,000 |
| **Shared fixed total** | **136,100** |

Per home: equipment 250; installation 100; expected replacement reserve 50 (4% annual failures x 5 years x 250); support 120 (2/month); electricity 91.98 (14 W x 8,760 h/year x 5 years x .15/kWh). Per-home variable total 611.98.

`TCO = 136,100 + 240*611.98 = 282,975.20` -> **$1,179.06/home**.

Home equipment/install is assumed prepaid, so shared capital alone requires provider financing. $6,000 is an allowance, not an APR quote. A fully financed deployment or expensive consumer debt needs repricing. Field/support labor is a paid share of a larger regional operation, not full-time staffing for a single village. If a standalone company needs salaried coverage, add it. Off-grid solar/autonomy upgrades are not hidden in the grid-plus-battery baseline.

## Starlink reference

Official advertised US monthly prices: 100 Mbps $55; 200 Mbps $85; Max $130, lower tiers area dependent. Reference assumes zero upfront equipment/installation, favoring Starlink. It is not a promise of free hardware at every address. Five-year terminal electricity at assumed 42.5 W costs $279.225 using .15/kWh. V5 official range is 35–50 W.

100 tier: 3,300+279.225 = **3,579.225**; 200 tier **5,379.225**; Max **8,079.225**. Tax, address-dependent offers and future price changes excluded. Baseline/100 ratio **32.94%**; quarter target ceiling **894.81**. Cost target fails.

## Invention-dependent stretch target

Shared five-year cost <=90,000; per-home lifecycle <=400; R&D allocation 30/home (300,000 across 10,000 customers): `90,000/240+400+30=805` -> 22.5% of the favorable 100 Mbps reference.

This requires ~46,100 shared savings and ~212/home savings, plus equivalent field performance. Manufacturing and maintenance innovation must deliver those reductions without weakening structure, support or backup capacity. The baseline mechanism budget itself is an unquoted fabrication estimate, not a commercial certified-mast price.

Charging all 300,000 R&D to the first 240 homes adds 1,250/home: total ~2,429/home. The first isolated deployment does not meet the mature-production quarter-cost claim.

## Adoption and prices

Provider cost excludes electricity paid directly by the home: variable 520/home, baseline provider floor ~18.12/home/month over five years. A $30/month equipment-included retail service costs the customer ~1,891.98 including electricity (52.9% of Starlink reference), not one quarter.

Provider five-year accounting break-even at $30/month: `ceil(136100/(30*60-520))=107` continuously paying homes. A whole-consumer-budget version including household electricity yields 115; it is not the correct provider revenue boundary. Adoption ramp, churn, collections, taxes and margin raise the requirement. Neither figure qualifies RF capacity.

The analytical quarter-cost subscriber count with unchanged baseline costs is ~482, exceeding the reference network's modeled 240-home capacity. Do not advertise that count as a build plan; additional sectors/sites/upstream change both costs and capacity.

## Other deployment cases

Sparse rural, 24 homes: shared capex 18,000; permits 1,500; upstream 1,000/month; annual sites 1,200; electricity 300; field labor 4,000; maintenance 1,500; insurance 600; systems 100; finance 4,000. Fixed 122,000 -> total 136,687.52, **5,695.31/home**. Worse than reference Starlink.

Disaster reserve, 50 homes/25 week-long deployments over five years: equipment 18,000; permits 2,000; upstream reserve 30,000; storage/insurance 7,500; base staff 10,000; deployment labor 25,000; replacements 3,000; finance 4,000; fuel 2,500; customer kits 12,500; installs 5,000; support 6,000 -> **125,500 total / 2,510 per home**. Assumes intact reachable terrestrial gateways. Not continuous five-year household service.

Matched activation example: five active Roam Unlimited months/year at 175/month -> 4,375/home over five years before hardware/standby. Disaster Auren estimate ~57% of this lower-bound comparator, not 25%. Starlink may still work when every reachable terrestrial gateway is destroyed; Auren cannot.

## Sensitivity / better alternatives

| Change | Baseline consequence |
| --- | --- |
| 120 subscribers | ~1,746/home |
| Upstream $2,000/month | ~1,504/home |
| 10% rather than 4% annual customer failures | +75/home before extra travel |
| 2.5-year customer-kit life | +250/home scheduled replacement |
| Additional $30k shared replacement | +125/home |
| Weekly 2-hour visits at four sites, $40/hour | ~1,369/home |
| Efficiency or usable spectrum halves | Additional capacity/cost or reduced service |

Early community uncertainty: approximately 900–2,000/home before concentrated R&D, not a statistical confidence interval. New mast certification, carrier construction or standalone staffing may exceed it.

Ordinary WISP wins when existing roofs/towers provide cheap LOS and good maintenance access. Fiber wins when a five-year all-in bid supplies better throughput/availability at comparable lifetime cost, particularly through existing ducts/poles or dense settlement. Obtain both bids; the invention does not deserve an exemption from alternatives.
